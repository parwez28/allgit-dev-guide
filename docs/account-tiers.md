# Account Tiers on allgit.dev

AllGit.dev organizes its inventory into four tiers based on account age,
history depth, and badge rarity. Choosing the right tier depends on your
goals, your budget, and how much instant credibility you need.

---

## Fresh accounts (0–6 months)

**What you get:** A clean GitHub profile with a verified email address and
little to no history.

**Who it's for:**

- Developers who want a **secondary account** separate from their main
identity.
- **Bot or automation accounts** where history doesn't matter but a verified,
working login does.
- Anyone who wants a **clean slate** they will build up themselves, without
paying for history they don't need.

**Why choose it:** Fresh accounts are the most affordable entry point and
are delivered instantly. If your use case doesn't depend on reputation —
testing, CI runners, sandboxed experiments — there's no reason to pay for
age.

---

## Aged accounts (1–2 years)

**What you get:** One to two years of real contributions, an established
contribution graph, and early badges such as Pull Shark.

**Who it's for:**

- **Freelancers** bidding on contracts where clients check GitHub before
hiring.
- **Job seekers** who need their profile to match the experience on their
résumé right now, not in two years.

**Why choose it:** This is the sweet spot for most buyers. You get immediate,
visible credibility — green squares, merged PRs, early badges — without the
premium price of the veteran tiers. For anyone whose income depends on how
their profile looks to strangers, this tier usually pays for itself quickly.

---

## Veteran accounts (3–4 years)

**What you get:** Deep, multi-year contribution history with rare badges such
as the Arctic Code Vault Contributor badge.

**Who it's for:**

- **Agencies and dev shops** that put developers in front of clients.
- **Founders** going through fundraising or partnership due diligence.
- **Senior developers** who want their profile to carry unmistakable
authority at a glance.

**Why choose it:** A three-to-four-year green history with rare,
time-locked badges is instantly recognizable to anyone who knows GitHub.
These profiles command respect before a single line of your own code is
reviewed. When the stakes are high — enterprise clients, investor meetings,
high-value contracts — veteran tier is the standard choice.

---

## Legend accounts (5+ years)

**What you get:** Half a decade or more of GitHub history, frozen-in-time
badges like Mars 2020, and often an existing follower base.

**Who it's for:**

- **Collectors** of rare digital artifacts.
- **High-stakes freelancers** competing for premium contracts.
- Anyone who wants the **strongest possible GitHub presence** money can buy.

**Why choose it:** Legend accounts are the rarest inventory. Five-plus years
of continuous history plus badges that can never be minted again make these
profiles closer to collectibles than commodities. Supply is limited by
definition — you can't manufacture five years of history overnight — so
availability varies.

---

## How to choose

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **Who will look at this profile?** Clients and recruiters notice age and
badges; bots and CI systems don't care.
2. **What are you optimizing for?** Immediate credibility (Aged), maximum
authority (Veteran), rarity and collectibility (Legend), or a clean
utility login (Fresh).
3. **Do you need something specific?** A particular creation year, follower
count, or badge combination can be sourced on request — message the
platform with your requirements and they'll check inventory.

Prices are listed in USD and inventory is described as hand-verified, so
every tier listing shows exactly what history and badges are on the profile
before you pay. No surprises.
